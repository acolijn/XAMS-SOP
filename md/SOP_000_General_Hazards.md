> [!NOTE]
> This sheet is not a procedure. It collects the hazards that apply to the whole
> XAMS installation, so that every SOP can name them without repeating them. Read
> it before working on the setup, and keep a copy posted in the laboratory.
> Each SOP adds the hazards specific to that procedure.

## 1. Hazards of the installation

> [!DANGER]
> **Asphyxiation - xenon or nitrogen released into the laboratory.**
> Both gases displace air and cause loss of consciousness without any warning
> sensation. Xenon is heavier than air and collects at floor level; nitrogen
> boiling off from a dewar fills the room from any height.
> Confirm the oxygen-deficiency monitoring is active before starting work, never
> work alone on the gas or cryogenic systems, and leave the room immediately if an
> ODH alarm sounds.

> [!WARNING]
> **Cryogenic burn - liquid nitrogen, cold gas, and uninsulated cold surfaces on
> the cryostat, the dewars and the transfer lines.**
> Contact with liquid nitrogen or a cold surface destroys tissue within seconds,
> and escaping cold gas can injure the eyes. A surface can stay dangerously cold
> for hours after a transfer, and a bottle carrying external ice is still cold
> inside.
> Wear cryogenic gloves and a face shield before touching any cold component,
> stand to the side of couplings while connecting or disconnecting, and do not
> handle a recovered bottle until the external ice has cleared.

> [!WARNING]
> **Stored energy - xenon storage bottles and the high-pressure side of the gas
> board.**
> A failed fitting or a hose released under pressure can strike an operator and
> cause serious injury, and pressure rises steeply as a filled bottle returns to
> room temperature.
> Confirm every connection is secure before opening a bottle main valve, open
> high-pressure valves slowly while watching the pressure response, and respect
> the approved fill mass per bottle.

> [!WARNING]
> **Electric shock - detector high voltage on the PMT and SiPM supplies, their
> cabling and the TPC electrodes.**
> Contact with an energised high-voltage conductor can be fatal, and cabling
> retains charge after the supply is switched off.
> Ramp to 0 V, disable the channel and switch off the supply before touching any
> cable or connector, and never make or break a connection while voltage is
> applied.

## 2. Personal protective equipment

| Activity | Required PPE |
| --- | --- |
| Any work on the gas system | Safety glasses |
| Handling LN2, dewars, transfer hoses or cold surfaces | Cryogenic gloves, face shield, apron, closed shoes |
| Xenon recovery and filling | As for LN2 handling, plus an O~2~ monitor |
| Work on detector high voltage | Safety glasses; no loose metal jewellery or watches |

PPE are kept in the main cabinet in the lab. Do not start an activity if the
required PPE is missing - report it instead.

## 3. If an ODH alarm sounds

- Leave the laboratory immediately. Do not stop to close valves or save data.
- Do not re-enter to help someone who has collapsed. The atmosphere that took them
  down will take you down too.
- Call the security desk on **020 592 6060** from outside the room.
- Call Auke-Pieter or Patrick from the contact list below.
- Re-enter only when the oxygen reading has recovered and an experienced operator
  agrees the cause is understood.

## 5. Who may work on XAMS

- Operating the gas and cryogenic systems requires a trained XAMS operator who has
  been briefed on cryogenics and oxygen-deficiency hazards.
- Working on detector high voltage additionally requires authorisation from the
  detector experts.
- Visitors and untrained staff may be present only under the direct supervision of
  a trained operator, and may not operate valves or supplies.
- Each SOP states any further competence it requires.

> [!NOTICE]
> An operator who is unsure of the hazard, the equipment state, or the next step
> can make an abnormal situation worse.
> Stop, leave the system in a safe state, and contact an experienced operator
> before continuing.
